01 / The project

From an idea to a documented prototype.

Sanitree is a student project exploring a conventional alcohol-based sanitizer formulation alongside the proposed addition of neem leaves. This website records the project rationale, process documentation, and work still needed to evaluate the prototype responsibly.

◎

The problem space

Hand hygiene relies on products with reliable performance and clear usage guidance. A botanical addition introduces a research question: can it be incorporated without compromising formulation quality or safety?

✦

Our approach

Document the base formulation, laboratory process, observations, and potential role of neem. Treat each benefit as a hypothesis until supported by appropriate testing.

⌁

Project objectives

Practice formulation documentation, understand ingredient functions, record prototype observations, identify validation needs, and communicate the work clearly during faculty evaluation.

Project status: This page describes a student prototype, not a certified or commercially validated product. No laboratory efficacy, stability, sterility, or skin-safety results are available yet.
02 / Scientific background

Know what each ingredient is meant to do.

Open each card to read the intended role and the questions that should be addressed before making performance claims.

◉

Ethanol

Primary antimicrobial ingredient

Ethanol is commonly used as the active antimicrobial ingredient in alcohol-based hand rubs. Its effectiveness depends on the final alcohol concentration, formulation, contact conditions, and correct use.

Validation question: Has the alcohol concentration in the finished prototype been measured using an appropriate method?

💧

Glycerol

Humectant

Glycerol is a humectant that helps retain moisture and is used in some hand-rub formulations to help limit drying. Its amount can influence feel and product properties.

Validation question: Is the final formula consistent and acceptable in use?

◇

Hydrogen peroxide

Formulation component

In the referenced WHO handrub formulations, hydrogen peroxide has a specific role in addressing contaminating spores in the bulk solution; it is not the primary hand antiseptic active ingredient.

Validation question: Does the preparation follow an applicable, verified formulation and controlled procedure?

❧

Neem leaves

Botanical research component

Neem (Azadirachta indica) is the botanical component proposed by this project. The composition of a leaf infusion can vary with plant material and preparation, so its effect on the finished formulation cannot be assumed.

Five neem leaves were used in this prototype. WHO guidance asks that any additive be clearly labelled and non-toxic if swallowed by accident, and advises against perfumes and dyes because of allergy risk.

Validation question: Does adding neem affect alcohol concentration, clarity, stability, contamination risk, skin compatibility, or measured antimicrobial performance?

Scientific communication ruleUse phrases such as “proposed,” “under investigation,” and “not yet tested” when evidence is not available. Avoid claims such as “kills 99.9%,” “sterile,” “non-toxic,” or “safe for sensitive skin” unless the exact finished product has suitable evidence.

07 / FAQ

Questions visitors may ask.

Short answers about the project, its limits, and next steps.

What is Sanitree?
Sanitree is a student-developed hand-sanitizer prototype project exploring a conventional alcohol-based formulation and a proposed neem botanical component. It is presented as a prototype, not a certified commercial product.
Why was neem selected?
Neem was selected as the botanical research component. Its inclusion raises questions that should be tested, including consistency, compatibility, stability, and any measurable effect on the finished formulation. Traditional use alone does not prove efficacy or safety in this product.
What does ethanol do?
Ethanol is the primary antimicrobial ingredient in many alcohol-based hand rubs. Performance depends on the actual final concentration and validated use conditions; a percentage claim should not be made without evidence for the finished product.
Why is glycerol included?
Glycerol acts as a humectant and can help limit the drying effect associated with alcohol-based formulations. The final amount and product properties still need to be checked.
Is the prototype proven to kill 99.9% of germs?
No. No efficacy test has been done yet, so this claim cannot be made. It would need suitable data for the exact finished formulation.
Is it ready for commercial use?
The website documents a student prototype. Commercial readiness would require appropriate formulation verification, quality control, stability and safety evaluation, efficacy evidence, compliant labelling, and review of applicable regulations.
Why is the prototype cloudy?
The cause has not been established. Plant material or water quality are possibilities. The next step is to repeat the batch with a filtered neem preparation, distilled water, and a control batch without neem.
What is the next step?
Agree on the formulation and validation plan with the faculty supervisor, record reproducible methods and batch details, complete appropriate tests, and update the website with actual results and limitations.
